  • Stokes 1845, On the theories of the internal friction of fluids in motion, and of the equilibrium and motion of elastic solids (Trans. Cambridge Philos. Soc. 8, 287; reprinted in Mathematical and Physical Papers, vol. 1, Cambridge, 1880, from p. 75)

    p. 80: the principle that the pressures depend only on the relative motion about the point and are unaffected by a rotation. p. 81: eq. (1), angular velocities of the fluid with the factor 1/2, and 'This is evidently an allowable definition, since, in the particular case in which the element considered moves as a solid might do, these quantities coincide with the angular velocities considered in rigid dynamics'. p. 82: axes of extension. pp. 83-84: the general motion compounded of translation, rotation, uniform dilatation and two motions of shifting. p. 85: p' = -2 mu e' for a single shifting. p. 86: eq. (5), and 'it must be taken as an additional assumption, and not a matter of absolute demonstration, that the effects of the two motions of shifting are superimposed'. p. 87: the second argument, called demonstrative. p. 88: eq. (6), the constant kappa set to zero.

  • Lecture 13 recording, Flow Between Two Parallel Plates

  • Reynolds 1883, An experimental investigation of the circumstances which determine whether the motion of water shall be direct or sinuous, and of the law of resistance in parallel channels (Phil. Trans. R. Soc. Lond. 174, 935-982)

    p. 943: critical velocities 'almost exactly in the inverse ratio of the diameters'; mu/rho proportional to P = (1+0.0336T+0.000221T^2)^-1; U_s = (1/B_s)(P/D), B_s = 43.79; range 5 C to 22 C; 'the critical velocity was very sensitive to disturbance in the water before entering the tubes'. p. 946: 'Up to these critical velocities the discharge from the pipes agreed exactly with those given by POISEUILLE'S formula for capillary tubes.' p. 948: v_c = (1/278)(P/D); 'much less than the critical velocity at which steady motion broke down; the ratio being 43.7 to 278'.

  • Nikuradse 1933, Laws of flow in rough pipes (VDI-Forschungsheft 361; NACA TM 1292, 1950)

    p. 1: Re = u d/nu with u the average velocity. p. 10: lambda = (dp/dx)(d/q), q = rho u^2/2 (eq. 1); laminar lambda = 64/Re (eq. 2); Blasius law holds for smooth pipes up to about Re = 1e5. p. 11: lambda = 0.316/Re^(1/4) (eq. 3); critical Reynolds number between 2160 and 2500.
